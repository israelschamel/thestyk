Find your stuff with a sticker.

Styk is a flat, flexible Bluetooth tracker you peel and stick onto laptops, notebooks, tools and bags. A built-in solar cell tops up its battery from everyday light, so there's no coin cell to replace.

Thin enough to go anywhere.

Most trackers are coins or rigid cards, so they hang off keyrings or live in wallets. Styk is a sticker. It sits flat on the thing itself, on a laptop lid, inside a notebook cover or on the side of a tool case.

What's inside a Styk

  • Solar window

    Turns room light and daylight into power and trickle-charges the battery.

  • Rechargeable cell

    A thin, flat battery that stores that energy for nights, drawers and dark bags.

  • Bluetooth chip

    Sends a small encrypted signal every couple of seconds that your phone recognizes as yours.

  • Buzzer

    A flat speaker that chirps when you tap Play sound in the app.

  • Adhesive back

    A strong peel-and-stick layer for smooth surfaces like laptop lids, plastic cases, metal and wood.

  • Protective top film

    Seals everything in against scratches, splashes and everyday wear.

How it works

  1. Peel and stick

    Peel off the backing and press Styk onto anything you don't want to lose.

  2. Pair it once

    Scan the code on the sticker with the Styk app to make it yours.

  3. Your phone keeps watch

    Whenever your phone is near your Styk, the app quietly notes when and where it last saw it.

  4. Find it

    Check when and where it was last seen, then tap Play sound to hear exactly where it is.

Styk has no GPS of its own. Location comes from your phone when it's nearby.

Your tags, in one app.

The Styk app shows every tag you own, how much charge it has and where your phone last saw it. It runs in your web browser, so there's nothing to download.

  • Play sound

    One tap and your Styk beeps, so you can follow the sound to it.

  • Last seen on a map

    Each time your phone sees a tag, the app saves the time and place, with directions back to it.

  • Battery and solar

    See how full each tag is and whether its solar cell is topping it up.

  • Left-behind alerts

    A warning when your phone loses touch with a tag while the app is open.

  • No account needed

    Your tags and their locations stay on your phone, not on a server.

Private by design.

A tracker should help you find your things, never help someone follow a person. Styk is being built with safeguards from the start.

Styk regularly changes the identifier it broadcasts, so a fixed signal can't be followed.

Questions

Will I ever need to charge it?

Styk is designed to top itself up from normal indoor light and daylight. If it spends long stretches in the dark, like the bottom of a drawer, the battery slowly runs down. Bring it back into the light and it recharges.

Does Styk have GPS?

No. Styk sends a small Bluetooth signal. Your phone notices it when it's nearby and records when and where that happened, which is what you see as "last seen" in the app.

Which phones will it work with?

The Styk app runs in your web browser at thestyk.com/app, with nothing to download. Real tags connect in Chrome on Android, and in Chrome or Edge on a computer. iPhone browsers don't allow Bluetooth yet, so on iPhone you can try the demo now while a native iPhone app is planned. We're also exploring the phone makers' own finding networks, which would let other people's phones help locate a lost Styk; that depends on each company's certification program.

Can someone use a Styk to track me?

Styk is being designed to prevent that. Its broadcast identifier changes regularly, and a Styk that travels with someone other than its owner for hours is designed to chirp so it can be found. We plan to follow the industry's standards for detecting unwanted trackers.

Is it waterproof?

It's being designed to handle splashes and rain. The final water-resistance rating will be confirmed in testing.

What will it cost, and when can I get one?

The target retail price is $19.95. Styk is in development, so final pricing and dates will come closer to launch. People on the launch list hear first.
